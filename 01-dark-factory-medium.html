Operating the SDLC in the Dark

When agents operate at machine speed, the entire lifecycle collapses, here's what replaces it.

by London Summers

6 min read

The "dark factory" is a manufacturing facility operating 24/7 with no humans on the floor. Robots work within an environment where humans are absent, except for design and exceptions.

Your SDLC can operate the same way. AI code commits trigger autonomous verification. Releases flow continuously through environments with zero human intervention. Incidents are detected and resolved by agents. Compliance is enforced by policy, not approval meetings.

But here's the uncomfortable reality: the agentic era doesn't make your current SDLC faster. It renders it obsolete.

The Paradigm Collapse

The prevailing narrative is that AI is a "10x developer tool". It's wrong. That framing assumes your workflow stays the same and only velocity improves. It doesn't.

The agentic paradigm isn't an enhancement, it's a replacement. When an agent generates 1000 pull requests per week and your team reviews 10 per day, you're not facing a bottleneck to optimize. You're facing a fundamental mismatch between agent speed and human governance.

The SDLC requires replacement, not optimization.

Five Core Paradigm Shifts

1. Ceremony Eradication

Sprints, story points, planning poker, CAB meetings, release trains. These existed to coordinate humans. Agents eliminate the need entirely. They don't exist because rituals are slow, they exist because humans are slow.

2. Compliance-as-Code

Regulatory controls bake directly into agent logic. Agents can't generate non-compliant code in the first place. Continuous, preventive, deterministic.

3. Fresh Environments

Agents iterate in isolated, just-in-time infrastructure. Ephemeral rings spin up on demand. No more weeks to test against stale environments.

4. Agentic Attestation

An agent's decision log becomes your audit trail. "Security check: passed. Architecture check: passed. Standards compliance: passed." Deterministic, logged, legal-equivalent to human review.

5. Observability as Primary Safety Layer

Code ships faster than humans can review it. Observability becomes your last line of defence. Automated anomaly detection and agent-driven remediation replace human code review.

From Human Led to Human Governed

The new core skill isn't coding. It's context engineering. Context engineers design and maintain the knowledge systems agents run on, the context lake of your architecture, policies, and patterns.

The new safety net isn't code review. It's observability. When agents ship faster than you can review, observability becomes your primary line of defence, with continuous telemetry and automated anomaly detection.

The new governance model isn't periodic audits. It's compliance-as-code. Regulatory controls bake directly into agent logic, preventive not detective.

Agents Collapse the Entire Lifecycle

Product discovery, design, architecture, implementation, deployment. The dark factory paradigm extends upstream through every phase. Customer feedback surfaces patterns automatically. Design emerges from feasibility constraints. Architecture decisions trace back to the problems they solve.

The entire lifecycle can run asynchronously, continuously, at machine speed.

The Question

Are you going to build a dark factory SDLC, or are you going to optimise a dead paradigm while your competitors operate at machine speed?
