The traditional SDLC was engineered for human speed. The moment agents operate at machine velocity, that entire operational model collapses.

The problem isn't that agents are fast. The problem is that governance, safety, and verification all fail when the thing building your system is 100x faster than the humans deciding what it should build.

The SDLC's Hidden Brittleness

Your current SDLC works because humans are slow. Planning, building, reviews, and testing took weeks. This serialization created natural checkpoints for correction.

An agent obliterates this timeline:

  • Ship working code in hours, not weeks
  • Generate 1000 PRs while you're sleeping
  • Iterate on architecture faster than teams can discuss it
  • Accumulate technical decisions humans can't audit in real time

Then apply the old SDLC: review 1000 PRs, hold planning meetings, get CAB approval. By then, the codebase evolved three times. The agent moved on.

This isn't a bottleneck. It's a paradigm collapse.

Where It Breaks First

Tickets become stale before they're started. The agent ships code while humans are still reading Slack notifications.

PR reviews vanish. You can't read inhuman-scale code. Rubber-stamping 1000 PRs teaches you nothing about what's being built.

Concrete scenario: An agent refactors a library across your codebase and submits 400 PRs overnight. At 10 minutes per review, that's 66 hours of work. You can't execute it. You rubber-stamp or languish. Either way, code review as a control mechanism is dead.

Ceremonies multiply. Sprints, planning poker, CAB meetings—they coordinated humans, not agents. Now they just slow humans down.

Governance breaks. You can't approve 2000 decisions per day. You won't even know what they are.

What Becomes Critical

Compliance-as-Code: Policy guardrails embedded in agent logic, preventing bad code upfront.

Context Lakes: A knowledge graph of your architecture and patterns. Without it, agents decide in isolation.

Fresh Environments: Ephemeral infrastructure spinning up on demand, not days/weeks to test.

Agentic Attestation: An agent's audit trail replaces human sign-off.

M&O as Primary Safety: Code ships faster than humans can review it. Observability becomes your last line of defence.

The Path Forward

Agentic development doesn't need faster ceremonies. It needs different ones. Not better gates. Better guardrails. Not human oversight. Machine-native verification.

That's a complete architectural shift.
